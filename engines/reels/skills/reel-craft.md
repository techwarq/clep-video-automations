# Reel craft (all styles)

## The first second decides everything
- Frame 1 already shows the subject AND words. Never open on a logo, black, a fade-in, or a slow reveal.
- The hook is a curiosity gap, a bold claim, a contrast, or a relatable pain — under 10 words.
- Something moves within the first 0.3 s (a punch-in, a slam, a cut).

## Pacing
- New visual information every 0.8–1.6 s: a cut, a punch-in, a word slam, a state change.
- Cut ON the spoken word (a couple of frames before its onset), never mid-word.
- Vary shot energy: wide → close → detail; push → drift → pull. Never the same move twice in a row.
- Hold longer (2–3 s) only on a payoff line or a title card, and keep a slow drift on it.
- Total length: memes 6–10 s, edits 15–35 s, explainers 20–45 s, talking heads 20–60 s.

## Captions
- Narrated reels always have captions: 1–3 words per chunk, timed to the words, big (60–90 px).
- Highlight the spoken word (colour, box, or full-opacity vs dimmed rest). Emphasis words get a different
  treatment (italic serif, yellow marker, accent colour) — max 1–2 per sentence.
- Captions must be readable over any footage: stroke, shadow, or a scrim — test on the brightest frame.
- If an on-screen graphic already says the words, hide captions for that beat.

## Type & layout
- One typographic voice per reel (max two families). Big, confident, tight tracking on headlines.
- Respect the safe zone: x 60–940, y 220–1540 for anything important.
- Full-bleed beats small: footage or colour fills the frame; never small UI floating in empty space.

## Motion
- Everything eases: `outCubic/outExpo` for arrivals, `inCubic` for exits, springs (`R.S`) for pops.
- Continuity beats cuts: when you can, let one object become the next (a dot becomes a button becomes a card).
- Cameras are never dead: a 3–8 % push/drift over every held shot; a 4–6 % punch on every cut.
- Exits are faster than entrances.

## Sound
- Music bed under everything (ducked under voice) unless the clip's own audio IS the joke.
- A designed SFX on every visual action: whoosh into transitions, pop/tick on text arrivals, hit on slams.
  Keep SFX gain 0.2–0.6 so they support, never shout.

## Endings
- End on the payoff, a punchline, or a loop point that flows back into frame 1. No "follow for more" cards
  unless the brief asks. A final line can hold ~1.5 s.

## Variety
- Never reuse a previous reel's content, palette or wording by default. Every reel's look, colours,
  layout and graphics come from ITS topic. The examples show technique, not content to copy.
