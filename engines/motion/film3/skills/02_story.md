# Story: a film that could only be about THIS product

## 1. Understand before you invent
From the site, find:
- what the product does, for whom, and the ONE hero workflow
- its real surfaces (the screens and components users actually see)
- its exact copy and real proof numbers
- its brand: colours, fonts, logo

Every picture in the film is one of the product's real surfaces or something from its users' real world.

## 2. The carrier (the most important decision)
Invent ONE object that exists in this product's world and can become everything else. The film is this object
morphing. Examples from the reference films:
- **Notch (Mac utility):** the black notch itself blooms into a music player, a meeting alert, a file shelf,
  a charging pill and a dashboard, then swallows the camera and becomes the logo.
- **Truecaller Business Chat:** the one real OTP notification among 24 spam ones becomes the chat card. That
  card morphs into an order tracker, a bill, a check-in pass and a feedback card, then becomes one row among
  thousands, which becomes the 450M+ counter.
- **TrueMile (logistics):** a delivery dot on a route becomes a message bubble, then a handled ticket.
- **Maritime (agents on servers):** a terminal line becomes a running agent, which becomes a sleeping container
  that wakes on demand.

The carrier changes shape, size, colour and content, but it is never replaced. Choose a carrier whose shapes
naturally fit the product: a pill, a card, a window, a notification, a message bubble, a row, a tile, a dot,
a document, the product's icon.

## 3. Events, not features (the difference between a launch film and a slideshow)
A slideshow shows feature 1, feature 2, feature 3. A film shows something HAPPENING: a crowd of problems arrives and
piles up → the one real thing is singled out → it is used and answers (tap → result) → it turns out to be one of
millions → the name. Every state change must be CAUSED by something on screen (a swipe, a tap, a drop, a number
reaching its value), never just "next slide".

## 4. Story shapes
- **Spine (15–35 s):**
  1. the moment of pain: concrete, one person, one situation
  2. the turn: the product used as an action (typed, clicked, dropped)
  3. watch it work: 3–5 concrete steps with real data
  4. the payoff: one answer or number, on the drop
  5. the name
- **Category takedown (40–90 s):**
  1. conflict: today's tools failing, concretely, piling up (15–20%)
  2. the product enters as the one thing that works (5–10%)
  3. demo: one command, then visible steps, then a concrete result (35–40%)
  4. proof: a real metric counting up (10%)
  5. breadth: fast kinetic list of what else it does, each item a carrier morph (10%)
  6. trust or objection: privacy, security or price detail (5–10%)
  7. name (5–10%)
- **Feature tour (any length):** one carrier, one feature per 1–2 bars. Each feature is the carrier blooming into
  that feature's real UI, doing one thing, then collapsing back to its idle shape. The finale shows everything
  at once on the drop.

## 5. Words on screen (no voiceover)
- Use 1–5 words at a time: human, specific, written in the product's voice.
- One line at a time, in clear space beside the carrier, never over busy UI.
- Use the product's real terms and numbers.
- Forbidden: "unleash", "supercharge", "seamless", "revolutionize", "the future of", "all-in-one",
  "next-level", "game-changer". A plain "Introducing" before the name is fine.
- The final line is the product's own promise: its tagline or a sharper version of it. Put the URL there too.

## 6. Demo data
Invent one consistent scenario: real-sounding names, times, amounts and IDs that add up. Use it in every state.
Never write "Item 1", "John Doe", "Lorem ipsum", "$1,000" or "100%".

## 7. Style signature (makes every film different)
Decide these before building:
- motion personality (crisp and technical, soft and warm, heavy and cinematic…)
- carrier and its shape vocabulary
- stage world (desktop, phone, browser, canvas, a space drawn in code)
- type treatment
- colour temperature
- sound palette character

Two films for different brands must differ in all of these.
